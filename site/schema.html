Data format

How the seat lists are structured, and the rules behind them. Schema hkperf-venue-seats/seatlist@0.4. Last updated 29 September 2026.

In short: one JSON file per seating layout, with a CSV beside it. Each file lists the parts of house, their rows, the blocks within each row and the seats in each block, in number order, with the marks the plan prints. Everything is a fact read from the operator's published plan, with its source. There are no seat coordinates: where rows sit around the stage is recorded as an arrangement, not a measurement.

Every file is checked when it is built: the seats counted must equal the totals printed on the plan.

1. Files

FileHolds
data/index.jsonthe list of seat lists, with names, region and date added
data/<id>.jsonone seating layout of one hall: the full record
data/<id>.csvthe same seats, one line per seat box, for spreadsheets

An id is the venue's short code and the hall, e.g. ktt-aud (Kwai Tsing Theatre Auditorium) or swcc-th (Sheung Wan Civic Centre Theatre). A room with several seating layouts has one file per layout, e.g. ekcc-lab-end and ekcc-lab-thrust. Text is UTF-8; the CSV starts with a byte-order mark so spreadsheet apps read the Chinese names correctly.

2. The venue index

{"venues": [
  {"id": "swcc-th", "name_en": "Sheung Wan Civic Centre Theatre", "name_zh": "上環文娛中心 劇院",
   "region": "Hong Kong Island", "added": "2026-09-26", "file": "swcc-th.json"},
  ...
]}

region is Hong Kong Island, Kowloon or New Territories, and must agree with the venue's district. added is the date the seat list was added to this project.

4. Parts of house, rows, blocks, seats

zones[]          a part of house: name, name_zh, optional sections, counted_in, count_includes, count_excludes
  rows[]         a row: its label, optional marks, inferred_numbers and note
    blocks[]     a run of seats between aisles
      seats[]    seat ids, in number order from seat 1's end
  • Parts of house (zones) are the areas the plan names and totals, e.g. Stalls 堂座, Balcony 樓座, Gallery. Where a plan names only the room, there is one zone with the room's name. sections record sub-areas the plan prints without separate totals (e.g. Front and Rear Stalls). Where the plan names two parts of house but the operator totals them together, both are zones and carry counted_in (see Totals).
  • Rows keep the label printed on the plan: A, AA, BD. Rows are listed front to back, as the plan orders them.
  • Blocks split a row where an aisle or a gap interrupts it, listed from seat 1's side. A block may name its side or area, and may record a blocked area the plan fills in solid, with the numbers it covers.
  • Seats are ids as printed: "12". Boxes printed without a number get a label in their row, W1, W2… or X1… (see below).

A real row, from the Sheung Wan Civic Centre Theatre:

{"row": "W",
 "blocks": [{"seats": ["1", "2", "3", "4", "X1", "X2"], "side": "left"},
            {"seats": ["W1", "W2", "9", "10", "11", "12"], "side": "right"}],
 "marks": {"X1": "X", "X2": "X", "W1": "W", "W2": "W"},
 "inferred_numbers": {"X1": "5", "X2": "6", "W1": "7", "W2": "8"},
 "note": "The left block ends with two solid black management boxes after 4; the right block opens with two
          wheelchair boxes before 9. The four boxes between 4 and 9 fill 5-8 exactly. No centre block."}

layout.seat_1_side says which end of each row seat 1 is at, as drawn with the stage at the top (left or right), and layout.note explains anything unusual: skipped numbers, missing row letters, rows that restart their numbering.

5. Marks

A row's marks map a seat id to one or more letters. Each file's top-level marks gives the plan's own wording for each letter it uses.

LetterMeaningIn the printed total?
Wwheelchair spaceyes
Xmanagement seat, kept by the venueno
Rrestricted sightlineyes
Llimited legroomyes

Letters combine: "RL" is a seat with both a restricted sightline and limited legroom.

6. Seats with no printed number

Plans often print wheelchair spaces and management seats as boxes with only W, a cross or a solid fill. Such a box keeps a label (W1, X1…) and gets a number in inferred_numbers only when that number is certain:

  • the gap between its printed neighbours fits it exactly, e.g. 20 · X X · 23 → 21 and 22; or
  • the operator's technical sheet names it, e.g. “Row S: S8, S9, S16 & S17”.

Otherwise it stays unnumbered, and the row's note says what is likely and that it is not printed. The CSV gives each seat's number_basis: printed, inferred or none.

7. Totals and the count check

printed_totals copies the plan's figures: {"Stalls": 172, "Gallery": 71, "Total": 243}. The rule that ties the seats to them:

Printed total = boxes on the plan − management seats (X). Wheelchair, restricted and limited-legroom seats are counted. This rule is our reading, not printed on the plans; it has matched every part of house of every plan so far.

count_check records the arithmetic for each part of house: boxes, each mark's count, boxes minus X, and the printed figure.

Totals from elsewhere. Where a plan prints no totals, totals_source says where the checked figures come from, e.g. the operator's technical sheet (Hong Kong Cultural Centre Grand Theatre).

Parts of house totalled together. Where the operator gives one figure for two or more parts of house the plan names separately, each of those zones carries "counted_in" with the name of that figure in printed_totals, and the zones are checked together against it. The Grand Theatre's Stalls 1 and Stalls 2 have "counted_in": "Stalls" (788); count_check then has an entry for Stalls listing its zones.

Seats a figure leaves out. Where the operator's figure leaves out a kind of seat the plan draws, the zone carries "count_excludes" with its mark and a count_note: Ko Shan Theatre's plan adds its six wheelchair spaces to the total separately, so its Stalls have "count_excludes": ["W"] and are checked as boxes − X − W.

Exceptions. Where the operator's figure for a part of house also counts its management seats, that zone carries "count_includes": ["X"] and a count_note explaining it; the check then counts every box there, and count_check records the exception. Nothing is adjusted to make a figure fit.

8. Orchestra pits

{"name": "Orchestra pit (10 × 2 m)",
 "rows_removed": ["AA", "BB"],
 "rows_basis": "stated",
 "rows_reasoning": "The technical sheet names the rows. AA + BB = 17 + 18 = 35 boxes ...",
 "seats_removed": 35, "total_with_pit": 447,
 "stated": ["seats_removed"],
 "source": {"url": "...swcc-th-lfe.pdf", "document_version": "V. 2026.03.09", "quote": "35 seats will be lost ..."}}
  • A pit option exists only where the operator states the seats it removes, or the capacity with it.
  • rows_basis is stated when the operator names the rows, and inferred when it gives only the number: the rows are then the run of front rows that gives exactly that number, and rows_reasoning shows the arithmetic and why no other run fits.
  • An entry in rows_removed is a row label, or, as in a bank, {"zone": "Stalls 1", "row": "C", "block": 2}: zone where row letters repeat across parts of house, block where the pit takes one block of the row. The Grand Theatre's curved pit lifts take whole front rows and the centre block of the row behind.
  • stated lists which figures the operator printed; the others are calculated.
  • A pit the operator describes without a seat figure is recorded in references, not as an option.

9. Sources and references

source identifies the plan exactly: publisher, url, file name, sha256 of the file read, the plan's printed version if any, and the credit line. If the operator replaces the file, the hash shows the data was read from an earlier version.

references are other documents from the operator, each with URL, title, printed version, hash, date retrieved, exact quotes and a note. They confirm totals, wheelchair numbers and pit figures, and record conflicts openly, e.g. a booking form that gives 592 where the plan and technical sheets give 596.

The data comes only from the operator's own published material. Ticketing and box-office data, and seat maps drawn by others, are never used.

10. Location

location holds address_en, address_zh, district, latitude and longitude, reproduced from LCSD's open data on DATA.GOV.HK. Each part names its source record (coordinates_source, address_source), and a script re-reads the open data to confirm them. Coordinates are LCSD's point for the building, not for the hall.

11. Banks: arrangement around the stage

In most halls every row faces the stage, and the map draws them one under another. Some rooms seat the audience on more than one side: a thrust stage, rows along the side walls, boxes. For these, layout.banks records the arrangement, still without coordinates. A bank is a group of rows, or single blocks of rows, on one side of the stage.

KeyHolds
ida name for the bank, e.g. stalls-left
sidefront, back, left or right of the stage (back is the far side, e.g. the opposite audience of a transverse or arena stage)
rowsits rows, nearest the stage first: a label for a whole row, or {"row": "A", "block": 1} for one block ([2, 3] for several); add "zone" where row letters repeat across parts of house
seat_1which end seat 1 is at: left/right for rows drawn level; downstage/upstage for upright rows
rows_runside banks: along the side (upright; default) or across (level, facing the stage)
in_lineupright side rows that follow one another down a wall, rather than sit side by side
afterside banks against the walls after a front bank, e.g. boxes between stalls and balcony
level_withside banks against a side wall, starting level with a named row of a front bank, e.g. boxes beside the stalls
besideside banks whose rows sit level with the same rows of a front bank, across the aisle
alignother side banks: level with the stage's front (downstage) or back (upstage), beside the front banks (house), or along the room (room)

The long-thrust layout of EKCC's The Lab, for example:

"banks": [
  {"id": "stalls-left",  "side": "left",  "rows": ["BA", "BB", "BC", "BD"], "seat_1": "downstage", "align": "upstage"},
  {"id": "stalls-right", "side": "right", "rows": ["DA", "DB", "DC", "DD"], "seat_1": "downstage", "align": "upstage"},
  {"id": "stalls-end",   "side": "front", "rows": ["AA", "AB", "AC", "AD", "AE", "AF"], "seat_1": "left"},
  {"id": "gallery-left", "side": "left",  "rows": ["BN"], "seat_1": "downstage", "align": "room"},
  ...
]

The viewer draws the stage in the middle, front banks below it, back banks above it (rows level and facing the stage) and side banks beside it. Order and neighbours follow the plan; distances do not. Every block of every row must be in exactly one bank. Files without banks are drawn with every row facing the stage.

12. Rooms with more than one layout

Flexible rooms publish one plan per seating layout, e.g. an end stage and a thrust stage. Each layout is its own seat list with its own totals; a layout's name is in its venue names, and its layout.note says which other layouts exist.

13. The CSV

One line per seat box, in the order of the JSON:

ColumnHolds
venue_idthe seat list's id
part_of_house, part_of_house_zhthe zone's names
row, blockthe row label, and the block's position in the row (1 = seat 1's side)
block_areathe block's side or area, where recorded
bankthe bank the block is in, where the layout has banks
seatthe seat id (12, W1, X2)
seat_number, number_basisits number, and whether that is printed, inferred or none
marksthe mark letters, then one 0/1 column each: wheelchair, management, restricted_sightline, limited_legroom
orchestra_pitsthe pit options that remove this seat

The viewer's download adds counted and not_counted_reason for the configuration chosen on screen.

14. What the build checks

A seat list is written only if all of these hold; otherwise the build stops with the reason.

  • Each part of house (or each group with counted_in): boxes − management seats = the printed total, and these add up to the printed overall total.
  • Each pit: the seats in its rows (or blocks) equal the stated loss, or give the stated capacity.
  • Banks, where given: every block of every row is in exactly one bank; sides, ends and references to other banks are valid.
  • Separately, a script confirms each address, coordinate and region against LCSD's open data.

Where a plan's PDF carries text, every seat number in the file was also matched one for one against it; scanned plans were read by eye from zoomed crops. Each script's header says which.

16. Drawing a map with an AI model

The viewer is one way to draw the data. If you would rather have a language model draw a seat map for your own use, here is an example prompt. Attach or paste one seat-list JSON file after it. It asks for a self-contained SVG and for the model to count its own output, which you should still check against the file's printed_totals.

You are drawing a schematic seat map from a structured seat list. The seat list follows the
schema hkperf-venue-seats/seatlist@0.4 (https://code.denniswu.org/hkperf-venue-seats/schema.html).
It is attached below. Produce one self-contained SVG, then a short check report.

WHAT THE DATA IS
- zones = parts of house (e.g. Stalls, Balcony). Each zone has rows; each row has blocks (runs of seats
  between aisles), listed from seat 1's end; each block lists seat ids in number order.
- Seat ids are printed numbers ("12") or labels for boxes printed without a number ("W1", "X1").
  If a row's inferred_numbers gives a number for such a box, you may show it in a tooltip, not on the seat.
- A row's marks map seat ids to letters: W wheelchair space, X management seat, R restricted sightline,
  L limited legroom. Letters can combine ("RL").
- There are NO coordinates. Do not invent positions, curves, rakes or distances. Draw a schematic.

LAYOUT RULES
1. If layout.banks is absent: put a stage box at the top. Draw each zone in order, with its name
   (name and name_zh) as a heading, and each row as one horizontal line under the previous one, centred.
   Within a row, draw blocks left to right in drawing order: if layout.seat_1_side is "right", reverse
   the block order and the seat order in each block, so seat 1 ends up on the right. Leave an aisle gap
   between blocks. If two neighbouring blocks skip numbers (e.g. 11 then 32), leave room for the missing
   seats instead of an aisle. A block with "side": "left" or "right" goes to that edge of the widest row.
2. If layout.banks is present, follow it instead. Each bank is a group of rows (or single blocks,
   {"row": "A", "block": 1}) on one side of the stage, listed nearest the stage first.
   - Put the stage in the middle. Front banks go below it, one after another, rows level and centred.
   - A row reference may name its part of house ("zone") where row letters repeat across levels.
   - Side banks (left/right) go beside it. With rows_run "along" (default) each row is an upright column
     and the bank's rows sit side by side, nearest the stage innermost; with in_line true they follow
     one another down the wall instead. With rows_run "across" each row is level, one behind another.
   - seat_1 says which end seat 1 is at: left/right for level rows, downstage/upstage for upright rows
     (downstage = the end nearer the front audience).
   - after: the bank sits against the side walls after the named front bank. level_with: it sits against
     the side wall, starting level with the named row of a front bank. beside: each row sits level with
     the same row of the named front bank, across the aisle; rows it lacks continue below.
   - align (other side banks): downstage = level with the stage's front, upstage = with its back,
     house = beside the front banks from their first row, room = centred along the whole room.
   Keep the order and neighbours exactly; distances are free.
3. Label every row at both ends (or at both ends of each column) with its row label.
4. Draw every seat as an equal square with its seat id inside; wheelchair seats show "W". Style marks:
   W filled blue, X crossed out and greyed (not counted by default), R with an inner outline,
   L with a light fill. Add a legend for the marks this file uses (file's top-level marks has the wording).
5. For each orchestra_pits entry, add a dashed outline around its rows_removed (the whole row, or only the
   block an entry names, found the same way as a bank's rows), labelled with its name and
   "rows stated" or "rows inferred" from rows_basis. If standing.places exists, draw them as round places.
6. Under the map, add this text, unchanged: "Not the venue's seat plan. This map is drawn from the seat
   data: a machine-assisted reading of the rows and seats of the venue. It is not the actual seat plan.
   For the actual seat plan, refer to the version published by the operator:" followed by source.url.
7. Title the map with venue.name_en and venue.name_zh. Credit: source.credit. Data: CC BY 4.0.

SVG REQUIREMENTS
- One <svg> with a viewBox; no external files or scripts; readable text (seat numbers at least 7px).
- Give each seat a <title> tooltip: row, seat, part of house, mark meanings, and any row note.
- Use data attributes on each seat: data-zone, data-row, data-seat, data-mark.

CHECK REPORT (after the SVG)
- Count the seat boxes you drew per zone, and how many carry X. Show: boxes - X = count, and compare it
  with printed_totals for each zone and for Total. They must match; if not, say which rows differ.
- List anything in the data you could not place, rather than guessing.

SEAT LIST:
[paste or attach one data/<id>.json here]
